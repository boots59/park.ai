Know what you’re seeing

A range, never a guess dressed as a count.

Counted: a person counted this bay less than 5 minutes ago. You see their number.
Estimate: the count is 5–60 minutes old. You see a range that widens as the count ages, and shifts if the bay's last two counts showed it filling or emptying.
Student report: someone who just parked tapped “plenty”, “a few” or “full”. It fills in when there is no fresh staff count, shows as a band (for example 1–4), and expires after 10 minutes. A staff count under 5 minutes old always wins.
Unknown: no count or report. Nothing is guessed. If there are enough past counts, the bay notes what is typical at this hour.
Level and deck totals: add up the bays that are known, and say how many bays that covers.
I arrive in…: pick a time and the verdict and “try first” are predicted for that moment. Counts will be older by then, so ranges widen, and a bay that was filling is projected forward.
Try first: the bays with the most open spaces at the low end of their range. The 3D view colours each bay the same way and updates as soon as a count is saved.

The range is sized to hold the real number about 9 times in 10, if its settings match this deck. They are placeholders until checked against real repeat counts; the staff field log runs that check. Age counts from when a survey started, because a walk takes minutes.

What is counted: open regular stalls: the ones not marked for faculty/staff or otherwise reserved, as the signs read on Nov 19. Not counted: faculty/staff, accessible (ADA), visitor, reserved, EV-only, motorcycle, blocked or coned spaces. An empty space is not always one you may use.

One bounded observation

Walk. Count. Complete.

Pick one bay and start. Walk the whole bay once and tap the big button for every open regular stall you pass (not faculty/staff, accessible or reserved). Don't mix partial walks or count a space twice.

Camera AI test (optional)

Walking and tallying is the reliable count. This tests the vehicle-detection model that could one day count from the deck's own cameras, using a phone photo as a stand-in. Photograph one section; the model counts the vehicles on this device and you check it. The photo is not saved or uploaded.

Counting rules

  1. Count only empty regular stalls. Skip faculty/staff, accessible, visitor, reserved, EV-only, motorcycle and blocked spaces.
  2. A completed survey replaces the bay's old count. It never adds to it.
  3. A survey open more than 20 minutes is rejected. Start again.
  4. Partial or cancelled surveys never reach students.
  5. Zero means you found no usable open spaces. Unknown means no count in the last 60 minutes.
  6. Recounting a bay 3–60 minutes after its last count gives students a trend, not just a number.

Field log

Each completed survey is logged with its real start and end time, the count, and for AI-assisted counts the model's number beside the confirmed one. No photos, names or plates.

Local demo only: this browser stores the simulated records. No staff login, shared server or audit log. Real use needs KSU approval and coordination between observers.

A credible one-deck pilot

Test the staff workflow and whether students understand the display before claiming to solve parking. Confirm with KSU Parking & Transportation: the real number of levels, eligible stalls per bay and permit type, and whether they would take part.

  • Built now: walk-and-tally bay counts, a field log, bay surveys, replace-not-add, timestamps from survey start, estimates with ranges, unknown states, a deck-level verdict, a "try first" recommendation, local storage, and a 3D view.
  • 3D model: made from public USGS lidar and USDA aerial photos. It shows where bays are. It does not detect cars or open spaces.
  • Estimates: between counts, a simple statistical model (trend from the last two counts, plus a range that widens with time). Its settings are placeholders until checked against real repeat counts.
  • Student reports: a parked student can report their bay as plenty, a few or full. Anonymous, one per bay per device every 5 minutes, and nothing stops a false report, so it is shown as a lower-trust band and never overrides a fresh staff count. (An earlier entry/exit tally was dropped: without a verified starting count it cannot produce a usable number.)
  • Cameras: not connected. KSU's Video Monitoring Standard (Sept 2024) routes live viewing and new cameras through the Department of Public Safety.
  • Privacy: no plates, faces, student identity or enforcement records.
  • AI: people walking and tallying is the reliable count today. An object-detection model (YOLOX-s, trained on the public COCO dataset) is included as a test of what the deck's own cameras could do later, with Public Safety's approval: it counts vehicles in a phone photo, on the device, and a person checks every number. Field testing on Nov 19 found two limits: one wide photo of a bay finds about 11 vehicles at most, and it also counts vehicles outside the bay.

This prototype cannot reserve spaces, validate a permit, prevent tickets or detect accidents. Use it to test an idea, not to make parking decisions.
